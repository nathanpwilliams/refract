[heading h2]
The triage habit

[text]
You now know the shape of the tool: a fluent drafter that predicts
rather than knows. That knowledge only pays off if it changes what you
do in the moment — so this lesson gives you a ten-second routine to run
before handing AI any task. Professionals who work well with AI aren't
doing anything mysterious; they're routing every task into one of three
lanes.

[heading h3]
The three lanes

[text]
**The draft lane — AI produces, you review.** Writing, summarizing,
reformatting, turning notes into prose, first-pass extraction from
documents you provide. The work is faster and the risk is controlled,
because nothing leaves your hands unread.

[text]
**The decide lane — AI informs, you decide.** Comparing options,
structuring trade-offs, briefing you before a judgment call. AI can lay
out the landscape, but the call itself — who to hire, which vendor,
whether to escalate — belongs to a person with accountability and
context the model doesn't have. Let it brief you; never let it vote.

[text]
**The verify lane — verified tools and sources required.** Numbers that
matter, facts entering a record, citations, anything regulated. Here AI
may assist around the edges — explaining a formula, drafting the memo
that carries the figures — but the figures themselves come from
spreadsheets, source documents, and systems built to be right.

[heading h3]
Three tasks, ten seconds each

[text]
Turning this morning's messy meeting notes into clean minutes you'll
read before sending: **draft lane.** You hold the source, you review
the output, and a miss costs a correction, not a consequence.

[text]
Choosing between two vendor quotes: **decide lane.** Paste both quotes
and ask for a side-by-side comparison of scope, price, and exclusions —
that part AI does brilliantly. Then close the laptop and decide,
because you know things the quotes don't say.
<!-- authoring note: quote-comparison example is deliberately
     refraction-friendly — every audience has a version of "two quotes" -->

[knowledge_check]
Q: Updating three figures in a client invoice before it goes out this
afternoon. Which lane?
- (a) Draft lane — AI is fast, and you'll glance at it after
  ✗ A glance doesn't catch a plausible wrong number — and an invoice
    is a record with consequences.
- (b) Decide lane — have AI recommend which figures to change
  ✗ There's no judgment call here to inform; there are numbers that
    must be right.
- (c) Verify lane — figures come from your billing system; AI can
  draft the cover note that goes with them ✓
  ✓ Right. The numbers travel from verified source to document
    untouched; AI works on the words around them.

[heading h3]
Run the routine under pressure

[scenario]
Situation: It's late afternoon. Your director needs a one-page
comparison of the three supplier quotes you've collected, in time for
tomorrow morning's decision meeting. The quotes are sitting in a folder.
You open your AI assistant. What's your move?

- (a) [poor] Ask the AI what these suppliers typically charge — it's
  seen a lot of pricing data, and you're short on time
  → The model has never seen these quotes or these suppliers' current
    terms. You'll get plausible market-shaped numbers presented
    confidently — and tomorrow's decision gets made on fiction. Time
    pressure is exactly when this shortcut is most tempting and most
    expensive.
- (b) [best] Paste all three quotes and ask for a one-page comparison
  table — scope, price, exclusions, open questions — then review it
  against the quotes and add your recommendation yourself
  → Draft lane for the table, decide lane respected for the call.
    Twenty minutes of work, grounded in the actual documents, ending
    with the one thing only you can add: judgment.
- (c) [poor] Paste all three quotes and ask the AI which supplier
  to choose
  → Grounded in the real documents — but you've handed over the
    decision itself, and making decisions is what professionals are
    paid to do. The model doesn't know your history with these
    suppliers, your schedule risk, or what your director cares about
    this quarter — and when the choice is questioned, "the AI picked
    it" is not an answer anyone accepts accountability for. Take its
    comparison; the recommendation is your job.

[callout key_idea]
Takeaway: before any AI task, ask — *am I asking it to draft, to
decide, or to be right?* Draft freely and review. Brief yourself, then
decide yourself. And when the answer has to be right, get it from a
source built to be right. That's the whole habit; the rest of this
course sharpens it.
